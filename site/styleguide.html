§2

Three type voices

Every string on a Yapper surface speaks in exactly one of these. If a label can't decide which voice it's in, it's probably two labels.

Shout — condensed caps for verdicts.
HERO 52–124Aa 40SECTIONAa 26CARDAa 16.5ROW TITLE
  • Web: Impact / Haettenschweiler stack · uppercase · letter-spacing .015em · line-height 0.94 · text-shadow 0 3px 0 black@55%
  • SwiftUI: .font(.system(size: N, weight: .black)).fontWidth(.compressed) on an uppercased string — SF Compressed Black is the native Impact
  • Used for: headlines, section titles, card titles, track/row titles. Never body copy, never lowercase.
Marker — the hand that labeled the tape
  • Web: Permanent Marker (embedded, Apache-2.0) → falls back to Marker Felt
  • SwiftUI: .font(.custom("Marker Felt", size: N)) — Marker Felt ships with macOS, zero bundling. Or bundle PermanentMarker.ttf via ATSApplicationFontsPath for an exact match.
  • Sizes 15–22 · usually paper yellow on dark or rec red on paper · rotate −0.8° to −2°
  • Used for: eyebrows, annotations, asides, track numbers, one-line jokes. Sparingly in-app — an annotation, not a system voice.
Mono — liner notes, body, data. The typewriter that filled in the J-card. Secondary runs at 60% cream, and labels go uppercase +0.16em.
  • Web: ui-monospace / SF Mono · body 13–13.5 px, line-height 1.75–1.8 · small 11.5 · labels 10–11 bold, tracking .12–.16em, uppercase
  • SwiftUI: .font(.system(size: 13, design: .monospaced)); labels add .kerning(1.4).textCase(.uppercase)
  • Used for: all body copy, settings descriptions, values, keycaps, footers. Digits in columns: .monospacedDigit().
§3

Two surfaces

Dark panels are crisp and rectangular; paper cards are torn, textured, and slightly rotated. Paper replaces the old blue as the "branded card" treatment in Settings. Ink text on paper, cream text on dark — never mix.

Dark panel

  • fill: vertical gradient panel-2 → panel
  • border: 1px line (cream @ 14%) · radius 4
  • shadow: 0 24 44 black@50% + inset 0 1 0 white@5%
  • dividers inside: 1px dashed line
  • rotation: none — dark stays square

Paper card

  • fill: 158° gradient paper-hi → paper 46% → paper-lo
  • texture: fractalNoise baseFreq .55 · multiply @ 16%
  • edges: torn clip-path (±0.5–2% jitter), no radius
  • shadow: 0 18 34 black@50% · rotation ±0.4–0.7°
  • masking-tape strip: cream @ 82%, screen blend, rotated
  • SwiftUI: gradient + noise image overlay (.multiply); torn edge via custom Shape — or skip the tear and keep gradient + grain + rotation

Atmosphere

Film grain sits over everything on the web page: SVG fractalNoise, baseFreq .82, 2 octaves, desaturated, overlay blend @ 10–13%. In-app: reserve grain for hero surfaces (About pane, Cassette shell) — skip it on dense list UI. A faint tungsten pool (radial-gradient, paper @ 7.5%) warms the area behind the hero object.

Chip / sticker & label-value pill

Paper fill, 2px ink border, radius 2, hard offset shadow 2px 3px 0 black@55% (stickers sit ON the page — no soft blur). Mono bold 10.5, uppercase, tracking .14em. The pill variant pairs a 9px label at ink@62% with a bold value — the app's About-pane treatment (VERSION 0.1.0). Ghost variant for passive metadata: transparent, line border, dust text, no shadow. SwiftUI caution: draw the hard shadow as an offset shape behind the fill — .shadow() re-renders label text as a ghost copy (see InfoPill in SettingsView.swift).

§7

Boldness budget

do

  • Spend yellow freely — it's the accent, not a highlight color
  • Rotate stickers, stamps, marker notes: ±0.4° to ±2°, always slightly different per element
  • Hard offset shadows on stickers; soft deep shadows on cards
  • Uppercase + tracking on every mono label
  • Keep the Minimal shell and menu bar plain native — contrast is the identity

don't

  • No pure #000 or #FFF — everything is warm
  • Never rotate body text or controls people operate
  • Max one acid element and one stamp per view
  • No bounces, no springs, no celebration moments — motion is mechanical
  • No grain over dense list UI; it lives on hero surfaces
  • Marker voice never sets more than one line
